Canadair Firefighting

Ten test drops to learn today's air. Five blind water drops to put out five fires.

Where a water load lands depends on drag, a wind that changes with altitude and the updraft above each fire, and none of it is known in advance. The agent must choose informative experiments, fit a model, then commit without ever seeing where its drops land.

How a mission works

1

Calibrate

Ten test drops over a calibration fire. Each shows where the water landed, how long it fell, and the wind at the aircraft's altitude.

10 test drops, noisy measurements
2

Build a model

Six numbers describe the air: drag, wind speed and direction, how the wind grows and turns with height, the fire's updraft. compare and simulate test a guess.

120 calculations in total
3

Commit

One water drop per hotspot, each with its own imposed heading, altitude and speed. No feedback: the agent never sees where the water lands.

Score per hotspot: 1 on the centre, 0 at 6 m or more

Where the points were lost

Missions cut short by a model provider's safety filter are left out here. For each episode two counterfactuals are replayed on the same world with the reference's own code: the reference fitting and aiming on the agent's test drops, then the reference aiming with the agent's final hypothesis. The differences split the gap to the reference into three decisions.
